# Arup — the deepfake video call — teardown

> 📚 Real case, confirmed by **Hong Kong police**, covered by CNN and
> Gulf News: [cnn.com](https://www.cnn.com/2024/02/04/asia/deepfake-cfo-scam-hong-kong-intl-hnk).
> Educational reconstruction of the concept — no real deepfake generated
> and no one's actual voice/image used.

## The museum's most unsettling room — and probably the future

Every other room entrusts the lie to text, static images, or a phone
voice. This is the first time you'll see, documented, an attack where
**your colleagues' actual faces and voices, in real time, on a video
call, were fake.**

## 1. The first warning sign — an email that did raise suspicion

It started with a phishing email impersonating Arup's UK-based CFO,
requesting a "secret" transaction. The employee, with good instincts,
**was suspicious** — email phishing alone wasn't enough.

## 2. The video call — `original/index.html`

To overcome that doubt, attackers convened a **video call** where the
employee saw and heard the real CFO and **several other colleagues**, all
AI-generated in real time. It wasn't one fake face — it was several,
reinforcing each other: if you recognize three colleagues at once, the
doubt dissolves.

## 3. The raw material of the deception

The deepfakes were built from **public video and audio** of real Arup
executives' previous corporate meetings and videoconferences — the same
kind of material any company uploads online or records internally
without considering this risk.

## 4. The outcome

The employee authorized **14 separate transfers**, totaling **$25
million**, to 5 Hong Kong bank accounts. Arup's CIO was clear afterward:
no company IT system was hacked — the entire attack was **social
engineering**, just supercharged by technology that until recently
seemed like science fiction.

## 🛡️ How to protect yourself (the museum's newest lesson)

- **Seeing and hearing someone on a video call is no longer sufficient
  proof of identity** for high-risk transactions — treat that as the new
  baseline.
- Establish an **independent verification channel** for large
  transactions: a pre-agreed passphrase, or confirmation through a
  completely different channel (not another video call).
- Be especially cautious if the request insists on **secrecy** or
  urgency — same red flag as classic BEC, just wrapped in far more
  convincing technology now.
