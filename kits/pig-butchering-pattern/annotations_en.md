# "Pig Butchering" — teardown

> 📚 Real pattern, documented by the **US Secret Service** (investment
> fraud unit) and the Department of Justice (cryptocurrency forfeiture
> actions): [secretservice.gov](https://www.secretservice.gov/investigations/investmentfraud-pigbutchering).
> Educational reconstruction of the fake trading dashboard — no real
> platform or cryptocurrency involved.

## The room that closes the museum — and finally breaks the pattern

Every earlier room shares something: the lie lasts minutes, maybe hours
at most. This one is different. Here, the deception is cultivated over
**months**, and the "kit" isn't a login page — it's an entire
**relationship**.

## 1. The name says it all

"Pig Butchering" comes from the Mandarin *Shā Zhū Pán*: **"fatten the pig
before slaughter."** The metaphor is deliberately blunt because it
describes the process exactly: the victim is "fattened" with attention,
affection, and trust over weeks or months, before the final blow.

## 2. The first contact — `original/index.html`

It starts with something trivial: a "wrong number" text via SMS, social
media, or a dating app. The victim replies out of politeness — and the
relationship begins there.

## 3. Patience as a weapon — months, not minutes

Unlike any other room in the museum, there's **no urgency** here. The
scammer builds a genuine-feeling relationship, with daily conversation and
apparent real affection, over weeks or months — until, quite naturally, an
"investment opportunity" in cryptocurrency comes up.

## 4. The fake trading dashboard — `original/dashboard.php`

The victim invests on a **platform that looks real**, with charts,
balances, and rising profits. At first, they're even **allowed to
withdraw** a small amount — a calculated move to build absolute trust
before asking for larger sums.

## 5. The final blow — once the pig is "fattened"

When the victim tries to withdraw a large amount, "fees," "taxes," or
outright silence appear. The platform disappears. So does the person
they'd been talking to for months.

## 6. The darkest layer of this case

Many operators at these scam centers, based in Southeast Asia, are
themselves **victims of human trafficking**, forced to run the fraud
under coercion — an additional layer of human tragedy behind the scam
itself.

## 🛡️ How to protect yourself from this

- Be wary of any online relationship that, sooner or later, drifts
  toward an "investment opportunity" — it's the most consistent signal
  across this entire pattern.
- No legitimate investment platform **requires** you to use the specific
  app your online "partner" recommends — always research independently,
  through separate channels.
- If you've never met that person in person or on a verifiable video
  call, treat any financial advice from them with maximum caution, no
  matter how long you've been talking.
