# The 419 fraud — teardown

> 📚 History documented by ExpressVPN, NordVPN, and Avast; 2024 loss
> statistics and the Lagos police operation that arrested 792 suspects
> that same year. Educational reconstruction of the email — with
> fictional names and figures.

## The grandfather of every trick in this museum

Before AOHell (1995), before the internet even existed, this scam already
existed — on paper, by letter. The 19th-century **"Spanish Prisoner"**
con promised the same thing: a trapped fortune, and you're the only
person who can unlock it, if you advance a small payment. The internet
didn't invent this fraud — it just made it **industrially scalable**.

## 1. The bait — `original/index.html`

An email contacts you, apparently at random, with an extraordinary story:
an exiled prince, an official with frozen funds, a widow with an
inheritance stuck in legal red tape. They need your help — and your bank
account — to release a fortune. In exchange, they promise you a generous
cut.

## 2. The mechanism — not a password, a ladder of payments

Unlike almost every other room, there's **no fake website asking for
credentials** here. The deception is far more direct: real payments,
each larger than the last, sent voluntarily by the victim. Each payment
"unlocks" the next obstacle — a fee, a bribe, a legal document — and
there's always one more.

## 3. Why the message sounds so absurd on purpose

The clumsiest emails, with obvious spelling errors and outlandish
promises, **aren't the scammer's mistake** — they're a deliberate filter.
Someone who believes it despite how absurd it sounds is exactly the
easiest victim to convince in the steps that follow.

## 4. The constant evolution — same skeleton, new costume

The "Nigerian prince" gave way to oil executives, officials with frozen
assets, soldiers with hidden treasure in conflict zones, charities
needing help after a disaster. Any current headline provides fresh
narrative material for the same old scam.

## 5. The outcome — the scale still surprises today

In 2024 alone, this fraud extracted **over $100 million** from American
victims. An estimated **3.4 billion** such emails circulate daily
worldwide. And these aren't lone scammers: a 2024 police raid in Lagos
arrested **792 suspects** in a single operation, revealing organized
offices with training programs for fraud "employees."

## 🛡️ How to protect yourself from this

- No real fortune ever needs **you** to advance money to release it —
  it's the most reliable signal in this entire pattern.
- Be wary of any contact combining an **extraordinary story + urgency +
  confidentiality** ("don't tell anyone").
- If you've already sent one payment and they ask for "just one more" to
  unlock the next step, **stop immediately** — that's the entire
  structure of the con, not a real obstacle.
